Theme analysis

1. EP694 is still not usable as a completed input

The resolver output is ready, and both YouTube and Social Worker Daily point to EP694. But local completion failed the market-watch gate: the episode has no structured notes, no tracking file, and no report HTML. This is exactly the case where the workflow should avoid narrative overfit and use previous state only.

2. Custom AI silicon remains a coexistence thesis

The strongest current evidence still points to coexistence: hyperscalers want custom silicon, but NVIDIA is trying to pull custom XPUs into an NVIDIA-centered rack-scale architecture through NVLink Fusion, NVHBM, networking, software, and packaging. That makes the US-tradable expression a basket of NVDA + AVGO + MRVL + TSM + MU + GOOGL, not a single replacement bet.

3. Broadcom: demand proof cleared, but valuation discipline becomes stricter

Broadcom's Q3 FY26 official release is high-quality evidence for AI ASIC and networking demand: revenue, AI semiconductor revenue, free cash flow and Q4 guide all strengthened. The hard part now is not proving demand exists; it is proving durable economics, customer concentration resilience, and a risk/reward entry that does not depend on continued multiple expansion.

4. NVIDIA: ecosystem gravity still matters

NVIDIA's Q2 FY27 official results keep the data-center demand bar high. More importantly for this framework, NVIDIA's AWS and MediaTek announcements add evidence that NVLink Fusion/NVHBM is moving from concept toward named ecosystem adoption. This supports the view that custom XPU growth can still be NVIDIA-adjacent if NVIDIA captures interconnect, memory architecture, networking, software, or rack-scale control points.

5. Marvell: proof lane remains alive, not fully de-risked

Marvell's Q2 FY27 results show record revenue and strong data-center growth, and IR materials point investors toward an October 6 Investor Day. This keeps MRVL as the most important custom silicon / optical proof lane after AVGO. The missing proof remains operating leverage, free-cash-flow durability, customer breadth, and whether growth requires expensive acquisitions or dilution.

6. TSM, MU, GOOGL, AMD: verification rather than narrative expansion

TSM stays the clean manufacturing anchor, but August revenue is not posted yet. MU's September 30 earnings call is the next clean HBM/memory-cycle checkpoint. GOOGL remains the buyer-ROI firewall: if cloud AI capex fails to convert into operating income, the supplier thesis weakens. AMD's Helios / Instinct story has improved official support, but it remains an evidence-gated alternative architecture lane.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • No FOMO after strong AI prints. Strong official data from AVGO/NVDA/MRVL increases evidence quality, but can also reduce forward return if the market has already capitalized it.
  • Demand proof is not the same as entry attractiveness. Upgrade the business thesis before upgrading position size.
  • Prefer liquidity. Use US-listed equities/ETFs first; thin OTC ADRs are signal instruments unless actual quote depth is acceptable.
  • Predefine invalidation. For any AI infrastructure exposure, define what evidence would reduce conviction: weak cloud ROI, margin compression, customer concentration, capex overruns, or ecosystem adoption failure.
