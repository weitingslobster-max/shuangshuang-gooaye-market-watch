Theme analysis

1. Alphabet as “digital landlord” — attractive, but proof-heavy

EP687’s most important US-market translation is Alphabet. The thesis is plausible because Alphabet owns search cash flow, Google Cloud, TPU/AI infrastructure, and a large data-center footprint. But the thesis only works if capex becomes visible cloud revenue and durable margin, not merely spending pressure.

Investment discipline: treat GOOGL as priority research / core watch. Add only on staged valuation discipline. Upgrade if Google Cloud revenue and operating margin continue improving while management can explain capex payback. Downgrade if AI model competition forces spending without measurable cloud or ad monetization benefit.

2. Cloud landlord comparison: MSFT, AMZN, META

MSFT remains the cleanest benchmark because investors can compare Azure growth, AI services, operating margin, and free cash flow. AMZN/AWS is the closest direct cloud-landlord comparison, especially if custom silicon and internal AI workloads improve returns. META is more fragile: it has scale, but investors still need clearer evidence that AI capex raises ad monetization or creates rentable infrastructure economics.

3. AI power, thermal, optical — still the best “picks-and-shovels” lane

EP687 reinforces the prior state: power, cooling, optical, and equipment are better aligned with 2026–2027 visible infrastructure demand than speculative application stories. VRT and ETN are cleaner US-listed power/thermal expressions. LITE/COHR/MRVL offer optical upside but carry customer concentration, margin, and inventory-cycle risk.

4. Apple as restraint comparator, not the preferred expression

Apple may show that the market is not automatically rewarding the largest AI capex budgets. But AAPL’s AI upside is indirect and depends more on iPhone replacement cycles, services, and feature adoption than on AI infrastructure monetization. Keep it as a benchmark for “capex restraint,” not as the main EP687 trade.

5. Local Taiwan names are signals, not US recommendations

Taiwan names such as Delta Electronics, cooling suppliers, and smaller local components companies are useful signals for AI power/thermal demand. They should not be converted into direct recommendations unless Weiting has a clean US-tradable route. For US-market purposes, prefer VRT, ETN, TSM, and selected liquid semiconductor names.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • Do not buy the slogan “digital landlord” without cloud margin and capex-payback evidence.
  • Prefer staged entries around primary-source proof events: earnings releases, 10-Q/10-K/20-F, monthly revenue, backlog/order commentary.
  • High-beta optical and power names should remain smaller satellites unless valuation resets or backlog/margin evidence becomes unusually strong.
  • Keep no-leverage discipline. If the thesis requires constant reassurance, size is probably too large.
