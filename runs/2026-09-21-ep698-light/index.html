Theme analysis

1) AI material spec-down rumor: useful risk signal, not proof of collapse

EP698’s CCL / PCB material discussion is best read as a margin and ASP durability test. Short term, supply-chain rumors about M4/M7/M8/M9 substitution or board-material downgrades can hurt local material stocks and challenge the idea that every AI-server generation automatically lifts component ASPs.

But the long-term counterpoint remains important: PCIe Gen6/7, CXL, higher-speed signaling, CPO and rack-scale interconnect still require quality materials and engineering. The market question is whether cost optimization merely delays or moderates the upgrade cycle, or permanently compresses the material value pool.

US-tradable handling: because the direct CCL/PCB names are mostly Taiwan local tickers, keep them as industry signals. Use NVDA / TSM / AVGO / MRVL / SMH / SOXX to monitor whether the broader AI infrastructure thesis is still supported by official revenue, margin and roadmap proof.

2) TSMC / AMD / NVIDIA pricing: separate who raises price from who keeps margin

The EP698 TSMC-to-AMD price rumor should not be written as confirmed guidance. It is still valuable because it asks the right question: in AI hardware, where does inflation land?

Investment rule: pricing power is bullish only if it survives through gross margin, cash flow and customer ROI. If costs are merely pushed into cloud capex without monetization, supplier revenue can stay strong while stocks still face valuation compression.

3) HBM stack choices: keep the shortage thesis, but segment use cases

EP698 raises low-stack / lower-high HBM as a way to improve yield, reduce cost, or serve edge-AI cases. This does not automatically weaken the data-center HBM thesis. It creates a segmentation checklist:

  • Edge AI / cost-sensitive systems: lower-stack HBM may be plausible if capacity and bandwidth needs are lower.
  • Training / high-end inference: require official evidence before assuming lower-stack alternatives can replace high-bandwidth, high-capacity HBM.
  • MU implication: stay constructive only if HBM mix, gross margin, and capex discipline hold in official disclosures.

4) Rubin / power-rack timing risk: long-term lane intact, near-term cadence less certain

EP686/EP688 built a constructive lane around data-center power architecture, 800V, BBU, PSU, power shelves and thermal infrastructure. EP698 adds a pacing warning: even if the roadmap direction is real, deployment timing can be slower if current Rubin power draw, cost optimization, or customer readiness does not force immediate conversion.

US-tradable implication: VRT / ETN / MPWR / ON / ADI remain useful watches, but upgrades should wait for backlog, margin, shipment, or customer-deployment proof rather than roadmap excitement alone.

5) Indie game publishing: interesting but not a public-equity trigger

EP698’s Tokyo Game Show / indie-game section is a good business observation: small teams often need publishing, legal, marketing, localization and deal-sourcing help more than raw capital. But it does not translate cleanly into Weiting’s US-tradable watchlist. TTWO is only contextual; it is not upgraded from this episode.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • Do not chase rumor-driven rallies. EP698 is specifically about rumors and cost-optimization narratives; it should make us slower, not faster.
  • Prefer proof over purity. CCL/PCB may be the pure local signal, but for US-tradable portfolios the better discipline is to monitor liquid names and ETFs.
  • Separate business quality from stock attractiveness. TSM/NVDA/AVGO can remain excellent businesses while still being poor entries after crowded rallies.
  • Use ETFs when single-name certainty is low. SMH/SOXX can express broad AI/semi exposure; CIBR can express AI-security without one-name valuation risk.
