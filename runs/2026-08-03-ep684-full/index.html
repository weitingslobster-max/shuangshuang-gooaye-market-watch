Theme analysis

1. Deleveraging is not the same as a broken AI thesis

EP684’s strongest investable insight is that crowded AI books can sell off because the owners need liquidity, not because every underlying company changed. Axios reported on 2026-07-30 that Situational Awareness sold its public-equities portfolio to Citadel after AI-stock losses. That is useful as a cross-check for EP684’s liquidation narrative, but the detailed AUM, performance, counterparty and position numbers remain news-supported, not primary-company verified.

Investment implication: do not buy merely because “it was forced selling.” Forced selling can create opportunity, but it can also reveal how much leverage was embedded in the prior valuation. The right response is to split the question into three gates: company fundamentals, balance-sheet/cash-flow durability, and tape stabilization.

2. AI fundamental firewall

The firewall is whether official company evidence still supports AI demand and monetization:

  • MSFT remains the benchmark because Azure, Microsoft Cloud, Copilot seats and earnings power give the cleanest public signal that AI can convert into revenue.
  • META is the caution case: revenue and engagement can be strong while capex, depreciation and free cash flow still pressure the equity story.
  • AMD is unresolved until its 2026-08-04 Q2 release; the Helios/Venice/MI450 story needs revenue, margin, software and customer-breadth evidence.
  • MU remains a constructive but cyclical HBM/memory expression; memory pricing/inventory can turn faster than narrative.
  • CRWV and BE are high-beta infrastructure/power expressions; both need financing, backlog conversion, margin and customer-concentration discipline before any upgrade.

3. MediaTek and AI PC / edge-AI signal

MediaTek’s official investor-relations page shows 2Q26 investor materials and monthly sales updates, and EP684 treats MediaTek guidance as a positive market signal. For this US-only workflow, however, MediaTek is not a primary trade candidate. It is an industry read-through for QCOM, AMD, AAPL, ARM, INTC, MU, and AI-PC / edge-device suppliers.

The right US expression is not “buy MediaTek through an illiquid proxy.” It is to ask whether edge-AI / AI-PC demand appears in official guidance from liquid US names.

4. Liquidity discipline and position sizing

EP684’s Taiwan disposal-stock discussion is directly relevant even for US investors: liquidity rules can turn a good thesis into a bad exit. The US-tradable lesson is to avoid oversized positions in thin ADRs/OTC names, avoid leverage, and prefer ETFs when a thesis is broad but single-name evidence is incomplete.
