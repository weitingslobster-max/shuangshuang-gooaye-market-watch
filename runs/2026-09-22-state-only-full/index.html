Theme analysis

1) AI infrastructure: official evidence remains constructive

The strongest conclusion today is that AI infrastructure demand is still visible in primary sources:

  • TSMC reported August 2026 consolidated revenue of NT$514.806B, up 53.3% year over year, keeping the foundry/HPC anchor thesis intact.
  • NVIDIA reported Q2 FY2027 revenue of $96.2B and Data Center revenue of $89.0B, with 75.0% GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins.
  • AMD reported Q2 Data Center revenue of $6.7B, up 107% year over year, driven by EPYC and Instinct demand.
  • Broadcom reported Q3 FY2026 AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B, up 221% year over year and 54% quarter over quarter.
  • Marvell reported record Q2 FY2027 revenue of $2.739B, up 37% year over year, with data-center growth accelerating to 46% year over year.

Investment implication: the demand lane is not broken. But the market already knows much of this. New money should still require either better entry prices or fresh proof that margins and cash flow keep pace with growth.

2) EP698 material spec-down risk: keep it as a margin-chain test

EP698’s CCL/PCB/spec-down discussion remains an important warning, but it is still mostly a local supply-chain signal rather than a clean US-tradable equity call. For Weiting’s scope, the right question is not “which Taiwan CCL name is purest?” but:

3) HBM and memory: constructive, but next proof gate is near

Micron’s latest official evidence supports the HBM lane: Q3 FY2026 was a record quarter, AI data-center demand remains central, and prepared remarks highlighted HBM4 momentum. The next hard gate is Micron fiscal Q4 results on 2026-09-30.

Stance on MU: keep as priority watch. Upgrade only if Q4 confirms HBM mix, pricing, margin and capex discipline. Downgrade if management signals lower-stack substitution, ASP pressure, or oversupply risk.

4) Power rack / data-center electrical: structurally strong, timing-sensitive

Eaton’s Q2 2026 result reinforces the electrical-infrastructure lane: Electrical Americas orders and backlog remained strong, and the broader electrification/digitalization cycle is intact. Vertiv remains a key AI-ready cooling and power watch, but the report keeps the same caution from EP698: roadmap direction can be right while deployment cadence is uneven.

Stance: VRT/ETN remain useful US-tradable expressions. ETN is the lower-beta infrastructure compounding lane; VRT is the higher-beta data-center thermal/power lane. Avoid treating every 800V/rack-scale roadmap item as same-quarter revenue.

5) Cybersecurity: AI-security proof improved

CRWD and PANW have stronger official evidence than the last state-only baseline:

  • CrowdStrike reported Q2 FY2027 revenue of $1.47B, ending ARR of $5.84B, record net-new ARR of $333M, and strong Falcon Flex adoption.
  • Palo Alto reported a strong FY2026 Q4/FY close and Q4 NGS ARR of $9.10B, up 63% year over year.

Stance: CRWD/PANW remain priority research. The AI-security thesis is more measurable now, but these are not automatic buys. Require valuation discipline, retention/ARR durability, and FCF conversion.
