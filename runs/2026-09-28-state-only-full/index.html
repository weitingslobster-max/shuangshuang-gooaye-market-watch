Theme analysis

1) Agentic AI and CPU demand: keep the proof-gate narrow

EP699's agentic-AI lane remains plausible: agents can increase orchestration, sandboxing, memory/I/O, tool-calling and general-purpose server work. That makes AMD worth monitoring through EPYC/Instinct demand and Data Center margins, while META remains an adoption and monetization signal. The discipline is unchanged: do not convert consumer-agent excitement or extreme CPU/GPU ratio claims into a base-case valuation upgrade without official demand and margin evidence.

2) DSP / optical interconnect: real bottleneck area, but price proof still missing

MRVL stays the liquid proof-gate for custom silicon, data-center networking and electro-optics. MXL remains a higher-risk, smaller DSP monitor. The thesis needs official confirmation of 800G/1.6T ramp, ASP/backlog, gross margin and FCF. CPO/LPO roadmaps remain a structural risk because a customer architecture change could compress traditional DSP value pools even if bandwidth demand rises.

3) AI infrastructure: constructive trend, strict stock discipline

TSM, NVDA, AVGO, MU, VRT and ETN remain constructive but entry-sensitive. The supplier chain only deserves higher conviction if the customer side keeps working: hyperscaler revenue, operating income and free cash flow must absorb capex, depreciation, leases and purchase obligations. If cloud ROI weakens, supplier visibility can become lower-quality backlog.

4) AI security: good thematic fit, measurable adoption required

Agentic AI can expand endpoint, identity, runtime, data, cloud and compliance attack surfaces. That keeps CRWD, PANW and CIBR relevant. The upgrade bar remains measurable: ARR/NGS ARR, retention, free cash flow, regulated-enterprise wins and product-level adoption — not just AI-security language.
