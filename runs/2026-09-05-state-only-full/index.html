Theme analysis

1. EP694 is detected, but not investable workflow input yet

The resolver found EP694 from both YouTube and the secondary-note index, but the local Gooaye artifacts were not generated. The correct workflow action is to preserve the current EP693-derived market state and wait for a completed EP694 report before adding new episode themes.

2. Custom AI silicon remains a coexistence thesis

The EP693 framework still holds: custom XPUs/ASICs are not automatically anti-NVIDIA. NVIDIA is trying to pull third-party accelerators into its rack-scale architecture through NVLink Fusion, NVHBM, networking, and software. This keeps the US-tradable universe centered on NVDA, AVGO, MRVL, TSM, MU, GOOGL and ETFs.

3. Broadcom: demand proof high; entry discipline stricter

Broadcom's official Q3 FY26 release is the strongest recent datapoint in this lane: Q3 revenue, AI semiconductor revenue, FCF and Q4 guidance all point to real demand. The investment question is now less “is AI ASIC demand real?” and more “how much of that future is already priced, concentrated, and margin-sustainable?”

4. Marvell: still the most useful proof lane outside Broadcom

Marvell's 10-Q confirms Q2 FY27 revenue growth and data-center strength from AI-related demand, plus the NVIDIA strategic partnership context. But MRVL still needs better proof of operating leverage, free-cash-flow conversion, customer breadth, and post-acquisition dilution discipline. The October 6 Investor Day remains a high-value checkpoint if it appears again on IR materials.

5. TSM and advanced packaging: do not front-run August revenue

TSMC's official monthly revenue page still shows July as the latest filled month; August is blank. That means the TSM trigger remains pending. TSM stays the cleanest US-tradable manufacturing/advanced-packaging anchor, but today does not clear a new revenue proof gate.

6. AMD alternative architecture lane improved, not decisive

AMD's Q2 2026 official release showed record revenue, strong data-center growth, and commentary around EPYC, Instinct and Helios. This improves the alternative rack-scale/inference watch, especially as a check on NVDA valuation and customer optionality. It still requires deployment economics, customer breadth, software stack adoption, and margin durability.
