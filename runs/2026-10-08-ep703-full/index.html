Theme analysis

1) EP703 risk-control translation: bullish anchor, bearish leverage

The core EP703 translation for U.S. investors is not “sell everything” and not “buy more beta.” It is barbell discipline inside equities: keep exposure where earnings proof exists, remove weak/non-confirming positions, and preserve liquidity for forced-selling moments. For U.S. accounts, this makes TSM/AVGO/MRVL/MU-type liquid instruments more usable than thin ADRs or local-only supply-chain names.

The practical rule is simple: if a name cannot confirm the thesis through revenue, margin, or relative strength while the broader AI/semi tape is strong, do not let the story become an excuse for illiquidity or leverage.

2) TSM: September revenue confirms the anchor, but Q3 margin is the gate

TSMC’s official September 2026 sales release is supportive: September revenue was NT$330.98B, up 31.4% YoY, and January–September revenue was NT$2.76T, up 36.4% YoY. This supports the EP703 idea that the market’s EPS anchor still has real fundamental backing.

However, revenue strength is not a complete investment case. For the ADR, the next decision gate is the Q3 call: HPC/AI mix, gross margin, overseas fab cost, capex intensity, and 2027 visibility. Keep TSM as core watch / no forced chase.

3) Supply-chain “mid-game break”: new screen, not a buy list

EP703’s most useful new research lane is the idea that some companies hurt by memory/CPU/component inflation or shortages may recover if supply stabilizes and volume returns. Because the episode did not name U.S. stocks, the correct market-watch output is a screen:

  • Prior calls explicitly blamed memory/CPU/components, freight, or shortages for margin/shipments.
  • Demand is not collapsing; customers are delaying or normalizing orders rather than disappearing.
  • Gross margin can improve from lower BOM cost, better availability, or mix normalization.
  • Balance sheet and liquidity are strong enough to survive a delay.

U.S.-tradable candidates to test, not yet upgrade: DELL/HPQ/HPE for PCs/servers/enterprise hardware; CSCO/GLW for networking/optical-adjacent hardware; SYNA/LOGI for end-device component/peripheral exposure. This lane should be verified with upcoming earnings transcripts before it enters the active recommendation table.

4) MRVL and optical: official deck validates the theme, but EP703 says manage risk

The official Marvell Investor Day material is now part of the source stack. Its slide outline includes custom opportunity expansion, data-center capex-driven growth, optical scale-up networks, and long-term revenue targets. That supports the existing MRVL/AVGO/MXL/LITE/COHR watch lane.

But EP703’s warning about supply-chain manias matters here. A larger TAM can become a larger valuation trap if expectations get pulled forward. MRVL remains priority research; MXL, LITE, and COHR remain high-beta monitors. Upgrade only when company-specific evidence shows revenue conversion, margin durability, customer breadth, and cash generation.

5) NeoCloud / “靠賽” examples: useful psychology, poor portfolio core

EP703 mentions NVIDIA, Nebius, CoreWeave, SpaceX and similar stories mainly as examples of luck, holding psychology, and theme rotation. That does not make NBIS/CRWV core holdings. These names require a separate credit-and-capex analysis: financing cost, GPU utilization, contract quality, depreciation, customer concentration, and cash burn.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • No leverage upgrade. EP703’s optimism is conditional; do not use it to justify futures, margin, or leveraged ETF exposure.
  • Prefer liquid proof leaders. TSM/AVGO/MRVL/MU/SMH/SOXX are easier to size and exit than thin ADRs or local-only names.
  • Do not chase optical sympathy. Wait for MXL Q3 and company-specific optical evidence.
  • Treat cost-relief names as a screen. Falling component prices can help margins only if end demand and shipment volume hold.
  • Keep cash as option value. Cash is not bearishness; it is protection against forced selling and a tool for future dislocations.
