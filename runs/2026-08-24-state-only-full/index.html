Theme analysis

1. MRVL / GOOGL: real strategic signal, still conditional economics

The EP690 lane remains the most important new hypothesis in state. Marvell's official 8-K and warrant exhibit frame the Google relationship as a commercial agreement tied to custom semiconductor products for Google's TPU ecosystem, with vesting tied to revenue thresholds. That is more investable than rumor, but less conclusive than realized revenue.

Investment read: MRVL deserves active research, but the risk is overpaying before the P&L confirms the deal. The right sequence is: filing signal → revenue recognition → margin quality → FCF and dilution math. Until that sequence is visible, this is priority research, not a clean accumulation signal.

For GOOGL, the customer-side question is more important than the supplier headline. Alphabet's official Q2 2026 materials should be monitored for Cloud revenue, Cloud operating income, capex, depreciation and operating cash flow. A better TPU supply chain only matters if AI infrastructure spend turns into durable Cloud and product economics.

2. AVGO: COT is a margin-pool question, not an automatic impairment thesis

Broadcom's official quarterly result path remains the first source for AI semiconductor growth and margin resilience. The EP690 COT framework creates a specific question: will hyperscalers unbundle enough procurement/design ownership to pressure turnkey economics, or will scaled IP, networking, custom silicon execution and software infrastructure preserve Broadcom's moat?

Stance: keep AVGO as priority research. Do not chase strength, but also do not downgrade mechanically from one MRVL/GOOGL filing. The next useful evidence is AI semiconductor revenue growth, customer breadth, backlog quality and segment margin durability.

3. TSM: quality anchor, but entry discipline matters

TSMC's monthly revenue page remains the cleanest routine check for the foundry/advanced-packaging anchor thesis. TSM is still the liquid US-tradable expression for many Taiwan-local supply-chain signals, but the report should avoid turning every upstream local name into a US buy idea.

Stance: core watch. Prefer pullback/staged entries, not momentum chase. Upgrade only when monthly revenue and HPC/advanced-packaging commentary keep supporting the thesis without valuation becoming stretched.

4. AI power and cooling: ETN/VRT lane stays high quality, but valuation-sensitive

Eaton and Vertiv are the cleanest liquid U.S. expressions of the AI data-center electrical/thermal bottleneck. Official Q2 materials should be used to verify orders, backlog, organic growth, margin conversion, and free cash flow. This lane is attractive because it is less dependent on one accelerator winner, but the stocks can still be punished if orders slow or valuation outruns backlog conversion.

Stance: priority research / staged entry only. The theme quality is strong; the discipline is to avoid paying peak multiples for cyclical infrastructure execution.

5. AMD, AMKR, MU and PLTR: useful proof gates, not automatic buys

AMD's official Q2 materials are the data-center/Instinct/Helios proof source. AMKR's official Q2 release is the packaging/test conversion source. MU remains the HBM/memory ceiling check even when no new primary memory update is available today. PLTR remains a value-add software monitor, but its valuation risk means growth and profitability must both improve.

Stance: keep them on the active dashboard. Let primary-source earnings and filings decide promotion/demotion; do not trade from theme labels alone.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • Core watch: GOOGL, TSM, NVDA, and diversified semi ETFs when single-name evidence is incomplete.
  • Priority research: MRVL, AVGO, AMD, ETN, VRT, AMKR, MU and PLTR.
  • Small satellite only: thin OTC passive-component expressions such as MRAAY if broker liquidity and spreads are acceptable.
  • Avoid / do not chase: headline-only MRVL moves, post-news AVGO overreactions, high-beta AI labels without primary-source cash-flow proof, and thin OTC proxies.
  • Portfolio process: require a primary-source trigger, then stage entries. No leverage and no automatic dip-buying.
