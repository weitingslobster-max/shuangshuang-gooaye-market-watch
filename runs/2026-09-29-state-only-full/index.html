Theme analysis

1) AI infrastructure demand is real; entry discipline still matters

The official numbers from NVDA and AVGO make the broad AI infrastructure trend hard to dismiss. NVIDIA's latest quarter still shows very large revenue growth, and Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue step-up is unusually strong. That supports keeping NVDA, AVGO, TSM, MRVL, MU, VRT and ETN active. It does not remove the need to check customer concentration, financing quality, hyperscaler ROI, and free cash flow conversion.

2) Agentic-AI CPU lane: AMD has better evidence, but the proof-gate is not closed

AMD's Q2 2026 Data Center revenue doubled year-over-year, and management tied demand to EPYC and Instinct. That is a real improvement for the EP699 agentic-AI CPU lane. The correct response is to keep AMD as priority research and monitor whether this becomes sustained Data Center margin power, not to turn extreme CPU/GPU ratio narratives into a base-case valuation.

3) DSP / optical interconnect: wait for MRVL's hard evidence

Marvell's calendar puts the next real gate on 2026-10-06 Investor Day. Product announcements around optical and AI connectivity are supportive, but the investment question is narrower: do 800G/1.6T ramps create visible revenue, ASP/backlog, gross margin and FCF? Until then, MRVL remains the liquid proof-gate and MXL remains high-risk / satellite-only.

4) Memory / HBM: tomorrow is a hard catalyst

Micron FY2026 Q4 on 2026-09-30 is the next clean test for HBM and memory-cycle discipline. The upgrade bar is high: pricing, mix, supply tightness, capex discipline and margin durability. Do not front-run the report just because the theme is attractive.

5) AI security remains plausible, but needs metrics

Agentic workflows increase identity, endpoint, cloud, runtime and data-governance risk. That keeps CRWD, PANW and CIBR relevant. The report should keep demanding ARR/NGS ARR, retention, free cash flow and measurable regulated-enterprise adoption before upgrading.
