Theme analysis

1. AI power architecture: thesis intact, valuation still separate

NVIDIA's official 800VDC material continues to anchor the EP688 thesis: rack power is moving from kW-era distribution toward MW-scale AI factory architecture. The important investment implication is not that every power-chain stock should be bought. It is that order/backlog/margin evidence from power infrastructure companies deserves higher priority than generic AI sentiment.

US expression: VRT, ETN, NVDA, plus narrower component monitors such as MPWR, ON, STM, POWI, NVTS, ADI and TXN. Discipline: prioritize system-level proof first. Component names need direct AI data-center content evidence before promotion.

2. AMD Helios / Instinct: proof improved, but still execution-gated

AMD's Q2 2026 release is the strongest fresh primary-source datapoint in this run. Revenue reached $11.5B, up 50% year over year, and Data Center revenue was $6.7B, up 107% year over year. AMD also stated that Helios begins to ramp and listed multiple AI-lab/cloud deployments or partnerships in the broader AI portfolio.

This supports EP688's open rack-scale inference lane. It does not prove AMD has caught NVIDIA in software ecosystem, margins, or platform control. The right stance is to keep AMD as priority research and watch whether Helios/MI400 converts into durable revenue visibility, gross margin, and customer breadth.

3. Eaton and Vertiv: same power thesis, different evidence quality today

Eaton's official Q1 2026 release shows strong electrical demand: Electrical Americas orders were up 42% on a twelve-month rolling average, and total Electrical Americas backlog was up 44% year over year. Management specifically attributes order strength to data-center momentum. That supports ETN as a lower-beta electrification expression.

Vertiv remains the purer play, but today's automated fetch hit a 429 / anti-bot block on the official Q2 IR page. Because primary-source verification failed in this run, do not upgrade VRT further on unverified numbers. Carry forward prior rating and recheck official IR or SEC filing access next run.

4. CBRS: keep the research object, not the risk budget

Cerebras matters because AMD's official release references the AMD/Cerebras inference collaboration, which validates EP688's architectural relevance. But CBRS remains a new Nasdaq listing with elevated IPO mechanics risk. Treat float, lockup, customer concentration, gross margin and durable cloud revenue as gates. No core allocation thesis yet.

5. Cloud ROI: demand-side proof remains the firewall

Power and compute suppliers can look attractive while cloud buyers are still absorbing massive capex. GOOGL, MSFT, AMZN and META remain demand-side proof gates: AI revenue, cloud operating income, depreciation pressure and FCF after capex determine whether infrastructure spend is sustainable. If cloud ROI disappoints, even strong order books can derate.
