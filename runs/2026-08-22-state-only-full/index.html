2. AI hardware demand is still supported, but supplier evidence is not enough

AMD’s Q2 2026 release supports the Helios/Instinct lane: total revenue reached $11.5B and data-center revenue was $6.7B, up 107% year over year. This strengthens the thesis that AI compute demand is broadening beyond a single vendor.

The important caution is that strong revenue is not automatically a strong stock entry. AMD still needs gross-margin durability, customer breadth and deployment proof; suppliers across the chain still depend on hyperscalers converting AI spend into profitable usage.

Portfolio implication: AMD stays priority research, not a chase. Use staged entries only if margin and deployment proof continue improving.

3. OSAT / advanced packaging remains an economics gate

Amkor’s Q2 2026 investor page lists the Q2 release, earnings presentation, transcript and 10-Q. The prior state’s conclusion still holds: AI/HPC packaging demand is real enough to monitor through AMKR and ASX, but the investable question is whether capacity and utilization become margin and free cash flow.

Portfolio implication: AMKR is the cleanest US-listed OSAT expression; ASX adds ASE industry exposure with ADR/liquidity awareness. Upgrade only on margin/FCF proof, not capacity headlines.

4. Power infrastructure is one of the cleaner non-semiconductor AI expressions

Eaton’s Q2 2026 release reported record sales, strong organic growth, accelerating orders and backlog. It specifically noted broad demand with data centers as a key growth driver. This supports the EP688/EP689 power-chain lane and keeps ETN as a lower-beta quality expression, while VRT remains the higher-beta data-center infrastructure name.

Portfolio implication: ETN/VRT remain active, but valuation discipline matters. Favor evidence of order-to-margin conversion over narrative about grid scarcity alone.

5. Hyperscaler ROI firewall remains the highest-priority risk dashboard

Microsoft’s FY2026 Q4 metrics page shows Microsoft Cloud revenue continued growing, and large commercial remaining performance obligations point to strong demand visibility. That is constructive for AI suppliers.

But visibility is not the same as self-funding. The EP689 financialization warning remains active: leases, purchase obligations, private-credit data-center debt and supplier financing can make near-term AI demand look safer than the customer economics actually are.

Portfolio implication: supplier exposure can stay on the watchlist, but risk should expand only when cloud/AI operating income and FCF confirm the spend. If FCF weakens while obligations rise, de-risk first.
