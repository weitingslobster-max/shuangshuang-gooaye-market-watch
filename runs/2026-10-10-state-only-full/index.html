2) TSM: still the anchor, but the next real decision is 2026-10-15

TSM remains the cleanest U.S.-tradable expression of the Taiwan HPC/EPS anchor. The official TSMC investor calendar and Q3 event page point to the 3Q26 earnings call on 2026-10-15. The upgrade/downgrade test is unchanged: HPC/AI demand, pricing discipline, gross margin, overseas fab cost drag, capex intensity, and 2027 visibility.

Until management speaks, the stance should stay 核心觀察 / core watch: maintain disciplined exposure if already owned, avoid leverage, and avoid adding solely on momentum.

3) MRVL/AVGO/optical: strategic backdrop improved, but valuation discipline matters more

Marvell's official Investor Day/custom-AI event backdrop reinforces the thesis that custom silicon, Ethernet/scale-up networking, optical DSPs, and interconnect are central to AI data-center scaling. This keeps MRVL and AVGO in the priority research lane and supports monitoring MXL/LITE/COHR.

But a stronger narrative also raises the risk of paying too much before cash proof. Upgrade conditions should remain hard: customer programs must turn into revenue; revenue must turn into gross/operating margin; margin must turn into free cash flow; and customer concentration must not create a hidden cliff.

4) MXL and optical satellites: wait for the Q3 call

MaxLinear's official IR calendar lists the Q3 2026 call for 2026-10-22. That is the right proof gate for optical/data-center ramp claims. MXL may have attractive optionality if Keystone/Rushmore/data-center connectivity ramps, but it should remain 小部位衛星 / small satellite only until revenue quality, margin, and cash conversion improve.

5) Cost-relief screen: useful, but not a buy list

The cost-relief idea remains valuable: lower memory/CPU/component costs can help hardware margins. But lower costs can also reflect weaker demand. DELL/HPQ/HPE/CSCO/GLW/SYNA/LOGI should be upgraded only if official filings/calls show both stable demand and margin improvement. Until then, this is a monitoring basket.
