Theme analysis

1) AI security and compliance: good theme, still needs numbers

CrowdStrike's latest investor materials emphasize Falcon platform modules, AI Detection and Response expansion, and partner integrations around AI gateways. Palo Alto's latest FY26/Q4 materials emphasize strong next-generation security ARR momentum and AI-powered security positioning. This supports EP697's thesis that model deployment creates a recurring security/compliance workload.

But the investment test is still strict: the theme must show up in ARR, net new ARR, pipeline quality, renewal rate, attach rate, or explicit customer adoption — not just product language.

Action: keep CRWD and PANW on priority research. Use CIBR if we want diversified cybersecurity exposure. Do not chase stretched valuation unless the next earnings cycle quantifies AI-security contribution.

2) Verification-compute thesis: plausible, not yet bankable

EP697's AI regulation / verification-compute idea remains plausible: audits, evals, red-team testing, monitoring and runtime controls should create incremental compute and software/security demand. However, this is still an inference. It is not yet official NVDA, hyperscaler, or software guidance.

Action: require primary-source confirmation from NVIDIA, hyperscalers, cloud customers, or regulators before upgrading this from “watch” to “position-size driver.”

3) Infrastructure winners: demand strong, concentration and capex still matter

Broadcom's Q3 FY26 release showed very strong AI semiconductor growth, and NVIDIA's Q2 FY27 filings/call materials continue to frame data-center growth around accelerated computing and AI systems. TSM, MU and MRVL remain important gates for packaging, memory/HBM and custom-silicon cycles.

The risk is not that the cycle is fake; it is that the market may already price in perfect ramps, high utilization, strong margins, and smooth customer financing.

Action: AVGO/NVDA/TSM stay high-quality watches, but do not add just because the theme is working. Look for pullbacks, margin proof, customer breadth, and cash-flow quality.

4) Cloud ROI firewall: the most important cross-check

The cloud complex remains the demand source for much of the AI supply chain. Primary filings and earnings releases continue to show both strong AI/cloud demand and very large capex / lease / infrastructure obligations. Oracle is the sharpest example: RPO and OCI demand are huge, but free cash flow, lease commitments, and financing quality need constant scrutiny.

Action: when ORCL/MSFT/AMZN/GOOGL/META rally on AI backlog or cloud demand, check free cash flow, operating leases, capex intensity, depreciation, and revenue-recognition timing before treating the rally as clean proof.
