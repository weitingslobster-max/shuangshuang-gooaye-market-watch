Theme analysis

1) Agentic AI and CPU demand: maintain the proof-gate

The EP699 conclusion still stands: agentic AI can plausibly increase CPU orchestration, memory, I/O, sandboxing and general-purpose server demand, but this must show up in official revenue, margin and customer-deployment evidence. AMD remains the cleanest US-tradable CPU/GPU challenger expression; META remains an adoption signal rather than a direct infrastructure trade.

What would change the stance: management commentary tying agentic workloads to material EPYC demand, data-center gross-margin improvement, or hyperscaler disclosures showing durable AI-agent monetization. App-store excitement, unsourced ratio math, or one-off anecdotes are not enough.

2) DSP / optical interconnect: still the freshest lane, still unproven on pricing

The useful EP699 research lane is 800G/1.6T interconnect and DSP. MRVL has the better scale/liquidity mix because it combines custom silicon, data-center networking and electro-optics. MXL has relevant PAM4 DSP exposure but should stay a high-risk monitor until production ramps, customer breadth and margin conversion are clearer.

What would change the stance: MRVL Investor Day evidence on 800G/1.6T ramps, backlog, ASP and FCF; MXL revenue-ramp proof; or customer roadmaps showing whether traditional DSP benefits or gets compressed by CPO/LPO transitions.

3) AI infrastructure: thesis intact, valuation discipline required

The state dashboard still supports constructive AI infrastructure demand: foundry/HPC, GPUs, custom ASIC/networking, memory/HBM, power/thermal and electrical infrastructure. But the market-watch process should separate business quality from stock attractiveness. Strong revenue does not cancel entry-price risk, customer concentration, supplier financing/backstop risk, or hyperscaler ROI pressure.

Portfolio implication: use staged exposure, liquid instruments and ETFs when conviction is broad. Avoid thin OTC expressions and leveraged catch-up trades.

4) AI security: measurable ARR/FCF first

Agentic AI can increase identity, endpoint, cloud, runtime, data and compliance surfaces. That keeps CRWD, PANW and CIBR relevant. The upgrade requirement remains measurable: ARR/NGS ARR, retention, FCF and regulated-enterprise adoption must improve; marketing language alone is insufficient.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • No leverage. The next few catalysts are close enough that forced timing risk is unnecessary.
  • No automatic dip-buying. Price weakness only matters if official evidence improves at the same time.
  • Do not confuse product exposure with pricing power. DSP, HBM and power components require ASP/backlog/margin proof.
  • Prefer liquidity. US-listed stocks and ETFs are preferred; OTC ADRs remain small/signal-only unless liquidity is clearly acceptable.
  • Keep cloud ROI as the firewall. Supplier revenue is strongest when hyperscaler revenue, operating income and FCF absorb capex, leases and depreciation.
