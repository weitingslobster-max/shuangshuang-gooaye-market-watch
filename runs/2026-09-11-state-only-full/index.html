Theme analysis

1. TSMC August revenue clears the nearest official proof gate

TSMC reported August 2026 consolidated revenue of NT$514.806B, up 53.3% YoY. Year-to-date revenue reached NT$3.38687T, up 39.3% YoY. This is exactly the kind of primary-source evidence the watchlist wanted: AI/HPC foundry demand appears strong enough to keep TSM as the cleanest liquid US-tradable anchor via TSM.

Investment read: this improves conviction in the business-quality lane, not necessarily the stock-entry lane. The next check is whether September revenue and Q3 earnings commentary confirm that advanced packaging/HPC strength is not being offset by overseas fab cost or margin pressure.

2. AVGO: demand proof plus financialization risk

Broadcom's Q3 FY26 release already showed exceptional AI semiconductor demand, with Q3 AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B, up 221% YoY and 54% QoQ. The newly filed Q3 FY26 10-Q adds a more nuanced risk signal: Broadcom disclosed an AI XPV platform with an initial $35B tranche led by a financial partner, tied to more than 1 GW of compute infrastructure, and a backstop arrangement for a customer's lease obligations.

Investment read: this can support visibility, but it also moves AI demand from simple product backlog into financing-structure analysis. For AVGO, the next upgrade condition is not more hype; it is evidence that AI revenue converts into durable gross margin, FCF, manageable customer concentration and low hidden-credit risk.

3. MRVL: operating proof improved, long-term proof still pending

Marvell's Q2 FY27 release reported record revenue of $2.739B, up 37% YoY, with data-center revenue growth accelerating to 46% YoY. Q3 revenue guidance of $3.150B ±5% points to further acceleration. That is supportive for the MRVL custom-silicon/optical lane.

Investment read: MRVL remains the highest-priority research lane because it sits where custom silicon, optical/connectivity and hyperscaler architecture overlap. But it still needs Investor Day detail on margin quality, FCF, dilution/warrant economics and customer breadth before a stronger portfolio label is justified.

4. NVDA / NVLink Fusion / NVHBM: ecosystem control remains the benchmark

NVIDIA's Q2 FY27 10-Q showed total revenue of $96.2B, up 106% YoY, and Data Center revenue of $89.0B, up 117% YoY. The MediaTek/NVIDIA announcement reinforces the EP693 thesis: custom XPUs may still be pulled into NVIDIA's rack-scale ecosystem via NVLink Fusion and NVHBM rather than simply replacing GPUs.

Investment read: NVDA remains the system-moat benchmark. The watch item is whether named customers and memory suppliers show production economics, not just architecture announcements.

5. MU and HBM: wait for Sep. 30, do not pre-judge

Micron has scheduled fiscal Q4 2026 results for 2026-09-30. That remains the cleaner proof point for HBM pricing, mix, gross margin, capex discipline and whether NVHBM changes bargaining power across the memory stack.

Investment read: MU stays priority research but cyclical. If HBM demand is strong but capex/working capital or price expectations become too aggressive, do not extrapolate a straight-line memory supercycle.

6. GOOGL / hyperscaler ROI: still the firewall

The supplier stack only works if buyers convert AI capex into cloud revenue, operating income and FCF. GOOGL remains a core ROI firewall because of TPU/COT optionality and Cloud economics. For AVGO/MRVL/NVDA/TSM, strong supplier revenue is necessary but not sufficient; buyer economics decide durability.

7. AI power chain: theme intact, proof still needed

VRT/ETN remain cleaner US-listed infrastructure expressions; MPWR/ON/ADI/TXN/VSH remain component/power-tree checks. The standard is unchanged: backlog and orders must become gross margin and FCF, not just revenue growth with working-capital drag.
