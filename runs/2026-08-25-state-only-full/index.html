Theme analysis

1. TSM: July revenue strengthens the anchor thesis, but price discipline still matters

TSMC's official July 2026 revenue report is the clearest fresh primary-source signal in today's run. July revenue rose to NT$467.58B, +5.6% sequentially and +44.7% YoY; year-to-date revenue through July rose 37.0% YoY. That supports the thesis that AI/HPC demand is still flowing through the foundry layer.

Investment read: TSM stays 核心觀察. It is the cleanest US-tradable expression for many Taiwan-local Gooaye supply-chain signals, but it should not be used to justify chasing every component stock. Prefer staged entries on weakness; upgrade only if monthly revenue, HPC/advanced-packaging commentary and margin/capex discipline stay aligned.

2. MRVL / GOOGL: real TPU ecosystem signal, not yet realized earnings proof

Marvell's official Aug. 19 filing says Marvell and Google entered a commercial agreement for custom semiconductor products tied to Google's TPU ecosystem, including accelerators, storage/network/memory interface controllers and near-memory compute; the same filing includes a warrant for Google to purchase Marvell shares at a specified exercise price. This is a real strategic signal, but it is still conditional.

For MRVL, the correct investment sequence remains: filing signal → product revenue/backlog → gross margin → FCF → dilution math. For GOOGL, the customer-side proof is whether AI infrastructure control improves Cloud revenue, operating income and cash-flow absorption after capex/depreciation.

Investment read: MRVL is highest-priority research, not a buy-on-headline. GOOGL remains the AI ROI firewall and a more resilient core watch than most supplier-only expressions.

3. AVGO: COT is a durability test, not an automatic share-loss call

Broadcom's official Q2 FY2026 release showed revenue of $22.187B, adjusted EBITDA of $15.244B, free cash flow of $10.262B, and Q3 revenue guidance of about $29.4B. That is strong enough that the EP690 COT narrative should be treated as a margin-pool question, not a reason to mechanically demote AVGO.

Investment read: AVGO remains 優先研究 / 不追高. The next evidence gates are AI semiconductor revenue growth, customer concentration, custom ASIC margin resilience and backlog quality.

4. AI data-center power: ETN and VRT remain constructive but valuation-sensitive

Eaton's Q2 2026 release reported 21% sales growth, 14% organic growth, strong orders/backlog growth in Electrical segments, and segment margins of 23.1%. Vertiv's Q2 2026 release reported net sales of $3.274B, +24% YoY, adjusted operating margin of 22.6%, operating cash flow of $1.1B and adjusted FCF of $925M.

Investment read: This is one of the cleaner second-order AI infrastructure lanes because it is less dependent on one accelerator winner. But it is not immune to cyclicality or valuation compression. ETN is the lower-beta core infrastructure watch; VRT is higher-beta and more execution/valuation sensitive.

5. OSAT, memory and AI software proof lanes

Amkor's Q2 2026 release reported record second-quarter sales of $1.90B, +26% YoY, with record revenue in Computing and Automotive & Industrial. That supports the OSAT/advanced-packaging bottleneck thesis, but AMKR still must prove that revenue growth converts into durable margins and FCF.

Micron's latest official fiscal Q3 2026 release keeps MU as the HBM/memory ceiling test: if the strongest memory scarcity name cannot sustain margins and valuation expansion, weaker component names deserve stricter multiples. Palantir's Q2 2026 filing/release keeps PLTR on the value-add software dashboard, but the stock remains valuation-sensitive; growth must show up with GAAP profit, FCF and customer ROI.

Investment read: keep AMKR, MU and PLTR active, but proof-gated. No automatic buying from theme labels alone.
