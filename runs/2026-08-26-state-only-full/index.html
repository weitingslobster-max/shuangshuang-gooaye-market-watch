Theme analysis

1. NVDA: same-day confirmation gate for the whole AI supplier stack

NVIDIA’s official Q1 FY27 release set a high bar: revenue of $44.1B and Q2 FY27 revenue guidance of about $45.0B, with management explicitly excluding H20 revenue because of export-control limits. Today’s post-close Q2 FY27 event is therefore not just an NVDA event; it is the near-term stress test for AI infrastructure demand, gross-margin resilience, China-related downside and downstream sentiment across TSM, AVGO, MRVL, AMD, MU, ETN/VRT and OSAT names.

Investment read: NVDA remains 核心觀察, but today is an event gate, not a pre-earnings chase. If the report confirms strong Data Center demand with stable margin and disciplined outlook, it reinforces the AI infrastructure dashboard. If margin, China/export controls, supply mix or forward guidance disappoint, reduce beta first and wait for cleaner entries.

2. MRVL / GOOGL: official Google signal, but tomorrow must start converting narrative into numbers

Marvell’s Aug. 19 8-K makes the Google relationship real: the commercial agreement covers custom semiconductor products tied to Google’s TPU ecosystem, and the warrant creates revenue-linked equity economics. That supports EP690’s COT/modularization thesis, but it does not yet prove near-term earnings power.

Tomorrow’s Marvell Q2 FY27 report should be read in sequence: data-center/custom-products revenue, gross margin, operating leverage, FCF, inventory/capex behavior and warrant/dilution detail. For GOOGL, the parallel question is whether TPU control improves Cloud economics enough to justify the supplier buildout.

Investment read: MRVL stays highest-priority research; GOOGL stays the customer-side 核心觀察. Do not chase MRVL on the warrant headline alone. Upgrade only if the next filings show revenue and cash-flow conversion without margin deterioration or excessive dilution.

3. AVGO: COT is a margin-pool durability test, not a mechanical downgrade

Broadcom’s latest official Q2 FY2026 release showed strong revenue, EBITDA and free cash flow, so EP690’s COT concern should be framed correctly: the risk is not that AVGO immediately loses relevance; the risk is that hyperscalers modularize procurement and squeeze turnkey custom-ASIC margin pools over time.

Investment read: AVGO remains 優先研究 / 不追高. The next evidence gates are AI semiconductor revenue growth, customer concentration, custom ASIC margin and backlog quality. If Broadcom continues to convert AI demand into cash with resilient margins, the COT risk stays manageable. If margins compress while MRVL-style COT relationships expand, demand becomes less investable for AVGO at high multiples.

4. TSM: quality anchor still works, but it should not justify chasing everything downstream

TSMC’s July 2026 monthly revenue was NT$467.58B, up 5.6% month-over-month and 44.7% year-over-year; January-July revenue rose 37.0% YoY. That remains the cleanest primary-source support for AI/HPC demand flowing through the Taiwan supply chain.

Investment read: TSM remains the cleanest liquid US-tradable expression for many local Taiwan signals. It should not be used as a blanket excuse to chase every component, OSAT or power name. Keep it as core watch; require entry discipline, monthly revenue continuity, HPC/advanced-packaging proof and overseas-fab cost discipline.

5. AI power, OSAT and memory: still attractive lanes, but cash conversion decides

The official Q2 evidence remains constructive in second-order AI infrastructure. Vertiv reported strong Q2 growth, margin expansion and raised full-year metrics; Eaton reported record Q2 results and strong Electrical orders/backlog. Amkor’s Q2 report supported the advanced-packaging/test bottleneck thesis with record second-quarter sales. Micron’s fiscal Q3 results kept the HBM/memory scarcity lane alive.

Investment read: ETN is the lower-beta infrastructure watch; VRT is higher beta and more valuation-sensitive. AMKR/ASX remain packaging/test proof lanes, and MU remains the memory/HBM ceiling test. None should be bought just because the theme is hot; require margins, free cash flow and capex discipline.
