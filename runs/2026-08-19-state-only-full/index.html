Theme analysis

1. AI power infrastructure remains the clearest EP688 lane

EP688 shifted the market-watch state toward 800VDC, power shelves, BBU/PSU, thermal infrastructure and rack-scale power distribution. The clean US expressions remain VRT and ETN, with NVDA as architecture setter and MPWR/ON/ADI/TXN/STM/VSH as component-level monitors.

The investment distinction matters: VRT is the cleaner pure-play power/thermal beneficiary, while ETN offers broader electrification exposure and less single-theme beta. If valuation is stretched, ETN or staged ETF/semi exposure is more disciplined than chasing the highest-beta name.

2. Hyperscaler ROI is the demand-side firewall

Supplier orders matter only if hyperscalers keep proving that AI capex converts into durable revenue, operating income and free cash flow. AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT and META should therefore be treated as infrastructure-demand validators, not just separate megacap holdings.

A constructive read is continued cloud demand and platform monetization. The risk read is that capex, depreciation and power costs outrun near-term AI revenue. This is why today’s stance stays “active watch / staged entries” rather than broad risk expansion.

3. AMD Helios / Instinct: improved proof, but not finished proof

AMD remains the key non-NVIDIA rack-scale compute monitor. The Q2 2026 evidence stack improved the credibility of Data Center and Instinct demand, and Helios remains relevant to the EP688 inference architecture lane. The open questions are commercial deployment breadth, software ecosystem proof, gross-margin durability and customer concentration.

4. CBRS stays event-gated

Cerebras is thematically important because it offers a different inference architecture path, but the stock remains unsuitable for core treatment without clearer public-company proof. The gates are filings, post-IPO liquidity, lockup behavior, customer concentration, revenue conversion and gross margin.
