Theme analysis

1. State-only day: keep discipline, do not invent a catalyst

Because EP683 is already incorporated and there is no new Gooaye input, today’s report should not manufacture urgency. The main job is to verify whether official evidence changes yesterday’s conclusions. It does not. The research posture remains: liquid US-tradable names first, no leverage, no automatic dip-buying, and no upgrade based on secondary rumor alone.

2. TSMC: the strongest official proof chain, but still no chase

TSMC’s official Q2 2026 release is the cleanest primary-source support in the current watchlist. Revenue was US$40.20B, up 33.7% year over year, gross margin was 67.7%, operating margin was 60.3%, and advanced technologies at 7nm or below were 77% of wafer revenue. Q3 guidance also pointed to higher revenue of US$44.6B–45.8B with gross margin of 65.0%–67.0%.

This reinforces TSM as a core AI/semi anchor and supports downstream AI supply-chain demand. The discipline, however, is unchanged: strong company quality does not automatically mean attractive entry price. Liquid winners can become funding sources in risk-off markets.

3. Memory / HBM: official evidence supports the lane; price discipline still matters

Micron’s fiscal Q3 2026 release reported record results and guided fiscal Q4 revenue to roughly US$50B with approximately 86% gross margin. Product commentary included high-volume HBM4 shipments for a lead customer’s platform and HBM4E development targeted for calendar 2027 volume production. Samsung’s 2026 materials also show HBM4 in mass production and HBM4E samples shipped to major global customers.

For US-tradable implementation, this keeps MU as the cleanest direct memory expression. But memory is cyclical and can be crowded. Upgrade only if margins, inventory, HBM allocation, LTAs/customer breadth, and price-volume reaction confirm that fundamentals still overpower positioning risk.

4. AMD / CPU-platform thesis: real official signal, limited inference

AMD’s official Microsoft announcement says Azure will deploy AMD Helios and that Helios combines MI455X GPUs, EPYC “Venice” CPUs, Pensando networking, and ROCm software in a rack-scale AI platform. AMD also says shipments to customers including Microsoft begin in 2H 2026. That is a meaningful official signal for AMD.

The limit is equally important: this does not prove a universal near-term CPU:GPU 1:1 architecture or a guaranteed margin outcome. Keep AMD in priority research, but make revenue conversion, gross margin, ROCm adoption, customer breadth, and deployment timing the upgrade gates. INTC remains a weaker, higher-risk CPU-platform monitor.

5. AI capex ROI: Meta caution vs Microsoft benchmark

Meta’s official Q1 2026 release already raised the full-year 2026 capex range to US$125B–145B, citing higher component pricing and data-center costs. The Q2 2026 event occurred on July 29, and the final official press release/transcript should be checked again when the investor site posts all materials clearly. Until then, META remains a caution monitor: AI capex may be good for suppliers while still pressuring the customer’s own equity if monetization lags.

Microsoft is the better benchmark because its official Q3 call described strong Azure demand, capacity constraints, large AI capex, and the free-cash-flow tradeoff. It is not a cheapness signal; it is the comparison case for whether high capex is being converted into durable revenue, margin, and cash-flow proof.

6. ASML / China DUV / CXMT: official evidence only

ASML’s Q1 2026 release supported a growth-year view but explicitly left room for export-control outcomes in the 2026 guidance range. That is enough to keep ASML on the priority research list, not enough to trade every China DUV/CXMT rumor. The trigger remains official policy, ASML order commentary, and credible company-level evidence.
