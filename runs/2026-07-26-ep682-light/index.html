Theme analysis

1. EP682 is a risk-control episode, not an all-in signal

The episode’s highest-confidence input is behavioral: when market selling is hard to explain, reduce leverage, protect cash, and lower trade frequency. For this workflow, that means upgrades need harder evidence than usual. A stock can be strategically important and still be a poor entry if market structure and valuation are fragile.

2. AI economics: inference cost is now a valuation input

EP682 reinforces that AI software is not classic zero-marginal-cost software. The right research question is whether AI usage becomes paid, sticky, and margin-accretive after token/inference, depreciation, and human-review costs. This is why GOOGL and META remain central: they test both monetization and capex productivity. Hardware beneficiaries (NVDA, AMD, MRVL, TSM, AMKR) also depend on hyperscaler ROI staying credible.

3. TXN improves the mature-semi evidence chain, but proof must broaden

TI’s official Q2 2026 release reported revenue of $5.46B, net income of $1.98B, and EPS of $2.14. That supports EP682’s “mature semiconductor recovery” line better than anecdotal supply-chain claims alone. The next step is breadth: ON, STM, ADI, VSH, AMKR, ASX, and mature-node foundry/OSAT data must show orders converting into margins and cash flow.

4. Memory: price-growth moderation is not automatically bearish

EP682’s memory point is useful: slower price increases can reflect supplier discipline or price normalization rather than demand collapse. Micron’s official FYQ3 2026 release still supports strong AI-memory demand and HBM momentum. The downgrade trigger is not “price increases slow”; it is inventory, guidance, gross margin, or capex behavior showing that demand is actually weakening.

5. AMD platform thesis remains active but entry-sensitive

Official AMD announcements support the EP681/EP682 AI-platform lane: Helios with Venice CPUs and MI450/MI455X GPUs is tied to 2H 2026 deployment language and customer/ecosystem commitments. This keeps AMD in priority research, but the underwriting test remains deployment, revenue, customer breadth, ROCm adoption, and margins.

6. Non-US/local names remain signals only

Nichicon, Samsung, Taiwan PCB/equipment and local passive/power names can help validate industry direction. They should not become primary recommendations in this report unless a clean US-tradable ADR, OTC ADR with acceptable liquidity, or ETF expression exists.
