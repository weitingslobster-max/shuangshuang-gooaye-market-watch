Theme analysis

1. Liquidity repair versus fundamental repair

EP685 should be read as a market-structure update. A fast rebound after forced selling can be rational when cash, short covering, and underexposed investors collide; it still does not answer whether AI capex produces enough economic return. For the US watchlist, this means PLTR, CRWV, BE, LITE, COHR, AMD, MU and NVDA are sentiment thermometers, while MSFT/GOOGL/META/AMZN/AVGO/TSM remain the better fundamental anchors.

2. PLTR and AI software COGS

PLTR becomes a useful high-valuation AI software signal because the official Q2 2026 shareholder-letter page is live and the episode frames the result as market-confirming. The investment discipline is not “PLTR up, buy SaaS.” It is: verify revenue durability, customer expansion, gross margin, adjusted/free cash flow, and whether AI token/inference costs change the old high-margin software model.

Working label: priority research / no chase.

3. HBM 8H/12H and TPU-delay noise

EP685’s HBM and TPU discussion is important because it helps prevent panic: product-mix changes and one-quarter roadmap slippage can happen inside a still-strong AI cycle. But the evidence level remains source-supported, not official. The right US-tradable expression is not a local HBM supplier; it is MU/NVDA/SMH/SOXX, plus AVGO/MRVL/GOOGL/TSM for custom ASIC and packaging read-through.

4. Optical and geopolitics

Optical remains attractive but fragile. LITE/COHR/MRVL/NOK provide cleaner US access than Chinese local optical names, yet the EP685 logic depends heavily on policy scope: restrictions based on country-of-origin are very different from restrictions based on ownership/entity-list status. This stays an active monitor, not a core allocation.

5. AI ROI firewall

The prior state’s firewall still matters: if hyperscalers show revenue, utilization, and FCF resilience, supplier strength is more investable. If they only show higher capex and depreciation, the rebound becomes lower quality. Microsoft’s official FY26 Q3 materials already showed AI infrastructure investment affecting cloud gross margin, while recent market/news commentary points to strong Azure momentum after FY26 Q4; the next task is to verify this in official FY26 Q4 filing/earnings pages as they become cleanly accessible.
