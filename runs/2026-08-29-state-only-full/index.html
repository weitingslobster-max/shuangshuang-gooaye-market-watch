Theme analysis

1. AI infrastructure proof is still real, but it is more crowded

NVIDIA's official Q2 release is the cleanest near-term proof point: revenue and Data Center growth accelerated materially, and the Q3 outlook excludes China Data Center compute revenue. That means the demand story is not dependent on China revenue in the near-term guide, but also means export policy remains a risk switch. Downstream themes — power shelves, optical, networking, packaging and memory — remain valid only where company-level evidence converts into margin and cash flow.

2. Marvell/Google COT lane: upgraded from headline to proof-improved

Marvell's Q2 record revenue and strong Data Center growth support the EP690/EP691 carryover thesis that hyperscalers may modularize custom AI silicon procurement. The right framing is not “Marvell replaces Broadcom.” It is: hyperscalers are negotiating the AI ASIC stack into smaller value pools. That can benefit MRVL while pressuring AVGO's turnkey margin narrative. The next hard gates are Marvell Q3 delivery and Investor Day on 2026-10-06, plus Broadcom's next AI semiconductor margin/backlog disclosure.

3. Customer ROI firewall matters more after hyperscaler capex disclosures

Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta all show strong AI/cloud demand, but also much higher infrastructure spend. Alphabet Cloud operating income improved sharply while quarterly free cash flow was negative after heavy property/equipment purchases. Microsoft posted strong cloud/Azure/Copilot metrics. Meta shows the caution case: revenue is strong, but Q2 capex and full-year capex guidance are extremely large and quarterly FCF was thin. This keeps supplier enthusiasm honest: AI capex has to show monetization, not just backlog.

4. Power, analog and packaging remain second-derivative beneficiaries

ADI's record fiscal Q3 and Eaton's record Q2/backlog language support the AI power-infrastructure lane. AMKR's Q2 and strategic AI/HPC packaging signals keep OSAT on the dashboard. These are not automatically “better than NVDA”; they are second-derivative expressions that can work when orders convert into margins and FCF. If capacity expansion only raises capex without pricing power, downgrade.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • Do not chase proof candles. Good earnings after a crowded AI run can still create poor forward returns if bought at stretched multiples.
  • Prefer liquid instruments. Use NVDA, GOOGL, AVGO, MRVL, TSM, AMD, ADI, VRT, ETN, AMKR, MU, SMH/SOXX. Keep MRAAY/MRAAF/ROHCY as liquidity-constrained or signal-only unless broker liquidity is proven.
  • Staged entry only. Best setup is a strong primary-source print, then a controlled pullback that holds above prior breakout/support with improving breadth.
  • Customer ROI before supplier extrapolation. Suppliers can report huge backlog while hyperscalers absorb negative FCF. If hyperscaler monetization disappoints, supplier multiples compress first.
  • No leverage. The state still treats deleveraging/forced-selling as an active overlay.
