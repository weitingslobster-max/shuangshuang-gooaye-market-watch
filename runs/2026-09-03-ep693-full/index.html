Theme analysis

1. NVIDIA × MediaTek / NVLink Fusion: ecosystem capture, not simple substitution

EP693’s useful insight is that NVIDIA may be building a bridge between hyperscaler custom silicon and NVIDIA’s own rack-scale platform. NVLink Fusion gives custom XPUs and CPUs a route into NVIDIA scale-up architecture, switches, chiplets, MGX systems, networking, and software. If adoption broadens, NVIDIA can still capture value even when a customer uses a semi-custom accelerator instead of only NVIDIA GPUs.

For US-tradable investing, MediaTek’s local ticker is mostly a signal. The cleaner expressions are:

  • NVDA for the architecture/control layer;
  • AVGO and MRVL for custom silicon and interconnect competition/coexistence;
  • TSM for foundry and advanced packaging;
  • MU for HBM/memory-cycle implications;
  • SMH/SOXX when the theme is too broad for single-name certainty.

2. NVHBM: high-value technical lane, still unproven economically

NVIDIA’s NVHBM announcement claims a different memory architecture: the memory controller moves into the HBM base die, potentially freeing XPU die area and improving bandwidth/power versus standard HBM4E. The investment value is not the headline spec alone. The real questions are:

  • Which memory vendors validate and ship it?
  • Who captures margin: NVIDIA, the XPU designer, memory vendors, or advanced packaging/foundry?
  • Does it become a standard for NVLink Fusion customers or remain a niche design?
  • Does it raise switching costs around NVIDIA rack-scale architecture?

Until those are answered, NVHBM is a priority verification queue, not a standalone buy signal.

3. Broadcom: Q3 print validates demand, but raises the hurdle

Broadcom’s Q3 FY26 release is the first big post-state trigger. Revenue, operating income, free cash flow, and AI semiconductor growth all came in strong. Q4 guidance also implies continued acceleration. This supports EP693’s “TAM expansion rather than zero-sum” framing: custom ASIC demand can be robust even as NVIDIA strengthens its own system moat.

But the higher the growth rate, the more important the downside checklist becomes: customer concentration, custom ASIC margin structure, customer-owned tooling economics, AI networking attach, and VMware cash-flow/debt contribution. A strong print upgrades the thesis quality; it does not remove entry discipline.

4. Marvell and optical interconnect: still priority, still proof-gated

MRVL remains the most important cross-check for “custom silicon plus optical.” EP693 does not independently prove Marvell’s economics; it confirms the lane matters. The next upgrade requires management to show that custom silicon and optical growth converts into gross margin, operating leverage, free cash flow, and a less fragile customer base.

5. TSM / advanced packaging / HBM base die

TSM remains the cleanest US-tradable manufacturing anchor. EP693 raises the value of tracking CoWoS/advanced packaging, XPU tape-outs, HBM base-die responsibilities, and monthly revenue. However, August revenue is not yet available in this run, so this is a forward trigger rather than a cleared proof point.

6. Market discipline: no-FOMO after strong proof

The most dangerous mistake today is to treat strong AVGO numbers plus exciting NVHBM language as permission to chase everything related to AI infrastructure. The better discipline is:

  • upgrade evidence quality;
  • keep position sizing strict;
  • prefer liquid instruments;
  • wait for valuation/entry setups;
  • avoid thin OTC proxies unless liquidity is explicitly acceptable.

  • NVDA: system quality remains exceptional, but export controls, customer ROI, and valuation still matter.
