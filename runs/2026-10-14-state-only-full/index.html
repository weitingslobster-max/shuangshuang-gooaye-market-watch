Theme analysis

1) TSMC is the pre-event decision gate

TSMC's official financial calendar lists the 3Q26 earnings conference and call for 2026-10-15. The monthly revenue page shows September 2026 revenue at NT$511.857 billion, up 54.6% year over year, and year-to-date revenue of NT$3.899 trillion, up 41.1%. That keeps the HPC/foundry demand backdrop constructive.

But revenue strength is not the whole decision. The call must answer the harder investment questions:

  • whether HPC/AI demand is still carrying pricing and mix;
  • whether gross margin can absorb overseas fab ramp costs;
  • whether capex remains disciplined relative to demand visibility;
  • whether advanced packaging / CoWoS constraints are easing in a margin-positive way;
  • whether management's 2027 demand language supports current valuation.

Because the call is tomorrow, adding risk today purely on pre-call optimism is not necessary.

2) Broadcom/OCP supports the EP704 interconnect thesis

Broadcom's official OCP Global Summit release says the company is presenting scale-up, scale-out and scale-across AI networking innovations, including Ethernet switches, NICs, PCIe components and integrated optics for ORV3 rack solutions. This fits the EP704 thesis that AI bottlenecks are spreading beyond GPUs into networking, optical links, rack architecture and system integration.

Investment interpretation: positive evidence for the lane, not a standalone buy signal. AVGO remains the cleanest liquid comparator because it has the broadest official product and cash-flow evidence among the U.S.-tradable names. But the next upgrade still requires earnings/filing proof: AI networking growth, custom silicon quality, customer concentration, working capital and FCF.

3) MRVL is strategically relevant but still proof-gated

Marvell's official filings describe exposure to optical DSPs, silicon photonics, advanced packaging, custom silicon and high-speed interconnect. That keeps MRVL highly relevant to the EP704 / OCP / 800G-1.6T lane.

The issue is not thematic fit; it is investment proof. MRVL should not be upgraded just because the map is attractive. The upgrade standard is:

  • recognized AI/custom-silicon and data-center revenue;
  • gross margin that improves rather than dilutes;
  • customer breadth beyond one or two hyperscalers;
  • working-capital discipline and free-cash-flow conversion;
  • evidence that 800G/1.6T DSP/optical ramps are nearer-term revenue, not only roadmap.

Until then, MRVL remains 優先研究 / priority research; no chase.

4) Optical high-beta names remain optionality, not core

MXL, LITE, COHR and AAOI can benefit if optical/DSP ramps become visible in shipments, ASP and margin. They also carry high volatility and proof risk. The current discipline is to separate the real AI networking bottleneck from low-quality sympathy trades.

Upgrade only when company-specific results show revenue, margin and cash conversion. Do not treat next-generation terms like 3.2T, TFLN, LPO/CPO or plasmonics as investable proof without official revenue timing.

5) Customer-side ROI remains the firewall

Supplier demand ultimately depends on hyperscaler and enterprise economics. GOOGL, MSFT, AMZN, META and ORCL should remain the customer-side firewall: capex, depreciation, AI revenue, cloud operating income and FCF must justify the supplier buildout.

If customer ROI weakens, supplier backlogs can still look good temporarily while stock risk rises. This is why AVGO/MRVL/TSM/NVDA should be judged alongside cloud-customer financials, not in isolation.
