Theme analysis

1. Custom silicon / ASIC economics

The investable tension is not “ASIC beats GPU” or “GPU beats ASIC.” The useful question is who captures economics when hyperscalers modularize custom silicon: Broadcom as incumbent turnkey/custom ASIC supplier, Marvell as custom/optical specialist, TSM as manufacturing and advanced-packaging anchor, or the hyperscaler customer itself.

For AVGO, today’s earnings should be judged on revenue visibility, margin durability, AI networking attach, customer concentration, and cash conversion. For MRVL, the Q2 FY27 revenue proof is encouraging, but the state still needs Investor Day detail on margins, free cash flow, dilution/warrant effects, and whether optical/custom silicon acceleration is broad or customer-specific.

2. Enterprise AI action layer

CRM is the cleanest near-term proof case after EP692 because it sits at the business workflow/data/action layer. The positive evidence is Q2 FY27 revenue, cRPO, and FCF strength. The unresolved issue is economic capture: if model partners, cloud vendors, or compute costs absorb too much value, enterprise AI usage may not translate into classic SaaS margin expansion.

The better expression may sometimes be diversified software exposure such as IGV rather than a single-name chase, especially when valuations run ahead of paid adoption proof.

3. NVIDIA system moat and supplier hurdle rate

NVDA’s Q2 FY27 result remains the benchmark. It supports the AI infrastructure demand base for TSM, MRVL, AVGO, optical, power, and data-center infrastructure names. But it also raises the evidence bar: suppliers must show not just AI exposure, but revenue, margin, FCF, and customer ROI.

4. Foundry / advanced packaging anchor

TSM remains a high-quality anchor, but today’s state-only run does not add a new monthly revenue data point. August revenue and HPC/advanced-packaging commentary are the next checks. Taiwan local supply-chain signals should not be converted into US actions unless a clean US-tradable instrument exists.

5. Optical and power infrastructure

LITE, COHR, MRVL, VRT, ETN, MPWR, ON, ADI, TXN, and VSH remain second-order beneficiaries. These are valid research lanes, but the discipline is strict: order/backlog, gross margin, cash flow, customer concentration, and valuation must confirm the narrative.

Valuation and entry discipline

  • AVGO: do not buy the story before the release. If AI revenue is strong but margin, customer concentration, VMware integration, or debt/cash-flow quality disappoint, the correct response is caution.
  • MRVL: treat as priority research, not proof of unlimited multiple expansion. Custom silicon can be valuable but can also become lower-margin customer-specific engineering.
  • CRM: enterprise AI is attractive only if paid adoption, retention, cRPO, and FCF move together.
  • NVDA: business quality remains excellent; entry should still be staged and sensitive to valuation, export risk, and customer ROI.
  • TSM: anchor quality is high, but geopolitical and overseas-fab cost risk require margin discipline.
  • OTC ADRs: MRAAY/ROHCY-style expressions remain liquidity-constrained signals unless broker spreads and dollar volume are acceptable.
